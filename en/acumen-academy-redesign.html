CONTEXT

Acumen Academy stands out in the e-learning landscape as the world’s first school for social change, founded by Jacqueline Novogratz to tackle poverty and advance global health.

In 2023, when I initiated this concept project on Start2Impact, the curriculum still included advanced cohort-based programs (Accelerators). Following a brand repositioning in May 2024, the organisation realigned its core focus around leadership development, the Alumni community (Fellowships/Foundry), and resources for high-impact startups (Founders Hub), archiving Accelerators among legacy programs.

Despite its ambitious mission, user research revealed a significant deficit in external brand awareness: the vast majority of respondents had never heard of the academy before the survey.

PROBLEM

The legacy Acumen Academy portal (2023) created significant friction in the desktop browsing and learning experience. The interface suffered from severe visual fragmentation, disorienting external redirects, and an overall absence of real-time support.

Inconsistent Navigation & Credibility

The Problem:

Dense copy with English jargon and poorly contrasted background imagery failed to convey the brand's authority.

The Impact:

Initial visitor distrust and visual fatigue while reading through the portal.

Outdated Filters & Visual Clutter

The Problem:

A course catalog with an outdated, rigid filter sidebar, paired with poorly scannable and cluttered cards.

The Impact:

Aimless scrolling and wasted time trying to filter short or free courses tailored to users' availability.

External Redirects & Support Gaps

The Problem:

Upon enrolling in a course, users were ejected from the site onto third-party platforms (Thinkific/NovoEd) with no assistance.

The Impact:

Disorientation, broken brand continuity, and platform abandonment at the first roadblock.

SOLUTIONS

Every visual and navigational solution was validated across the 3 usability test tasks and a portion of Gabriele's user flow (one of my User Personas), refined with insights gathered directly from the field to deliver a seamless, zero-friction interaction.

Lightened copy with bold text as visual anchors and hover tooltips on English jargon to reassure users of the institution's credibility.

Redesigned filter sidebar featuring icons and optimized course cards with tags and clear durations to quickly locate relevant learning resources.

Social Login via Gmail, a "Get Help" dropdown menu accessible directly from the Dashboard, and a centered Live Chat overlay born out of Pilot Test feedback.

Course bookmarking, Dashboard transition, and completely unified in-platform learning under the cohesive Acumen Academy architecture.

PROJECT CONSTRAINTS

The decision to focus the redesign on the desktop layout stems from the fact that target users primarily browse and take courses on laptops and desktop screens. Designing this unified in-platform e-learning experience required balancing a 1440px desktop layout (with a 12-column stretch grid) with WCAG 2.1 AA/AAA accessibility standards for digital inclusion.

All while aligning with the new brand identity, preserving legacy typography families (AvantGarde-Demi and Caecilia LT Pro), and maintaining Design System consistency across all sections.

THE DESIGN PROCESS

1. HEURISTIC EVALUATION

I kicked off the project with a heuristic evaluation based on Nielsen's 10 Usability Principles to identify barriers on the legacy website by testing the desktop experience.

Learnability (Initial Ease of Use) 4 / 5

The site is immediately clear and straightforward to grasp, though a few inconsistent links create some initial confusion.

Efficiency (Speed & Workflow) 3.5 / 5

Navigation is easy thanks to clear menus and footers, but continuous redirects to external websites slow down the overall experience.

Memorability (Ease of Recall) 4 / 5

The clean structure makes returning intuitive, though the absence of a personal area makes retrieving saved courses tedious.

Errors (Error Handling & Prevention) 2 / 5

Too many technical roadblocks disrupt the user flow, including broken links (404 errors), non-playing videos, and vague error notices.

Satisfaction (Visual Appeal & Comfort) 2.5 / 5

Copy layout and typography are neat, but overly harsh colors and text superimposed over busy images cause eye strain and hinder readability.

OVERALL SCORE ⭐ 3.2 / 5

In summary: The website has a solid structural foundation and is easy to understand, but urgently required a redesign to fix contrast issues, eliminate broken links, and host courses directly on-platform without offboarding users.

2. ACCESSIBILITY AUDIT

Behind Acumen Academy’s visual interface lay invisible yet critical roadblocks for many users: unreadable low-contrast copy, keyboard-inaccessible elements, and form fields lacking visible labels.
To bring the platform up to international WCAG standards, I mapped out the most critical issues on the legacy portal and translated them into actionable, production-ready design solutions.

Contrast & Readability

Observed Issues (As-Is):

Key copy in the Hero and text overlaid on images had contrast ratios below the WCAG minimum (4.5:1). Excessive use of bold styling across long paragraphs and headlines.

Proposed UX Solution (To-Be):

Increased contrast ratios to at least 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text). Added semi-transparent dark overlays and established a balanced font weight hierarchy.

Keyboard Navigation & Focus States

Observed Issues (As-Is):

Dropdown arrows in the Foundry menu, carousel navigation, and course filters were inaccessible via keyboard Tab key. Focus indicators were weak or completely missing.

Proposed UX Solution (To-Be):

Integrated tabindex="0" on all non-native interactive elements. Defined crisp, high-contrast CSS focus indicators.

Semantic HTML Structure

Observed Issues (As-Is):

Improper heading hierarchy (e.g., impact metric percentages coded as H1). Related navigation items and footer social icons were not marked up semantically as lists.

Proposed UX Solution (To-Be):

Restored proper heading levels (single H1 per page, sequential H2/H3). Converted menus, feature lists, and footer social links into semantic lists (<ul> and <li> tags).

Forms & Input Fields

Observed Issues (As-Is):

Newsletter footer form lacked persistent visible labels, relying solely on placeholder text. Form inputs had inconsistent styling and lacked autocomplete attributes.

Proposed UX Solution (To-Be):

Introduced persistent visible <label> elements (following Material Design guidelines). Unified visual styling and added programmatic autofill attributes.

3. COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS

To design an impactful solution, I stepped back to explore how leading players across the e-learning and social impact sectors navigate similar challenges, conducting a targeted competitive analysis of three direct competitors (Global Health Learning Center, Philanthropy University, HREA) and two indirect industry leaders (Coursera and TED-Ed).

The objective was to identify best-in-class market standards and turn them into actionable redesign opportunities.

4. USER RESEARCH & SURVEY

To validate my target audience hypotheses, I distributed a 12-question quantitative survey to a sample of 30 real users.

Target & Budget

The core target audience consists of young professionals aged 25–34. An impressive 80% are eager to invest in their social impact education, but favor an accessible price point, ideally between €0 and €19.

UX Goal: Full pricing transparency and strong visual emphasis on free course offerings.

Brand Potential

A staggering 93.3% of respondents were unfamiliar with Acumen Academy. However, once introduced to its mission, 37% expressed a clear intent to actively participate in the platform.

UX Goal: A high-impact Homepage that communicates the brand value proposition within the first 3 seconds.

User Experience

Top user priorities include a simple, intuitive interface (86.7%) and authentic student reviews (80%) to validate course quality.

UX Goal: Zero visual noise, flawless color contrast, and prominently displayed student reviews across all key pages.

Transitioning from mid-fidelity wireframes to the final UI prototype enabled us to test intuitive pathways, clear visual hierarchies, and scalable components prior to user validation.

Final UI (To-Be)

VALIDATION & USABILITY TESTING (UXArmy)

Following Figma prototyping, I ran a remote unmoderated Usability Testing session with 5 target participants to observe spontaneous behaviors via Think Aloud protocol (thinking aloud with microphone and webcam active), evaluating navigation, filter sidebar usage, and real-time support discoverability.

  • The Pilot Test: Prior to the official launch, the pilot test revealed a structural limitation in the floating chat bubble, which obstructed content on smaller desktop screens. I replaced it with a centered modal overlay featuring background dimming, accessible via "Contact Us", while reframing Task 3 neutrally.
🔴 BEFORE (Pilot Test - Bottom-Right Floating Bubble)
Observed Issue: The floating bubble and its bottom-right popup proved intrusive on smaller desktop screens, causing visual overlap with text and active controls.
🟢 AFTER (Official Test - Centered Live Chat Overlay)
Implemented Solution: Live chat redesigned as a centered overlay with a dimmed backdrop, accessible from "Contact Us" and triggered via "Chat Support" for an unobstructed browsing experience.
  • The 3 Tasks & Post-Test Survey: Operational testing protocol and qualitative post-test questionnaire.

🎯 TASK 1 (Credibility & Brand)

Locate key information about the organisation to assess and validate its credibility.

🎯 TASK 2 (Guided Search & Course Filters)

Find a free "Business Strategy" course under 5 hours in duration, using only the sidebar filters.

🎯 TASK 3 (Rapid Support)

Identify the fastest way to contact support in the event of a critical roadblock.

📋 POST-TEST SURVEY QUESTIONS

1. Trustworthiness: “How trustworthy did you find this organisation?"

2. Friction Points: “What was the most confusing part of the experience?"

3. Intent: “Would you consider learning with Acumen Academy?"

RESULTS & IMPACT

The three tasks evaluated institutional credibility, search filter efficiency, and speed of accessing immediate support. The data validates the complete achievement of primary desktop UX goals:

Task 1 — Credibility & Impact 0% Critical Errors

100% Trust Rating: All participants understood the organisation's mission and confirmed brand authority thanks to impact data and institutional logos.

Task 2 — Catalog & Filters 100% Completion

4/5 "Very Easy" Rating: The sidebar filter system enabled users to successfully isolate short, free courses even with the search bar disabled.

Task 3 — Immediate Support 1m 15s Resolution

Zero Drop-offs from Technical Roadblocks: Accessing assistance proved swift and seamless thanks to the redesigned Live Chat overlay.

Key User Behaviors & Mental Models

Cross-analyzing Think Aloud sessions and heatmaps surfaced several established usage habits to account for in the interface. Among these, three core user behaviors guided the subsequent iterations:

Fidget Clicking & Visual Anchors:
4 out of 5 users repeatedly clicked empty whitespace to pace their reading. Dense text blocks cause fatigue, whereas bold keywords guide the eye purposefully.

Visual Browsing vs. Filters:
In Task 2, 40% of participants (2 out of 5) bypassed the filter sidebar, visually scanning the grid and using the word "Business" in the title as an immediate visual trigger.

Footer as a Support Refuge & Complex Terminology:
In Task 3, 100% of users navigated straight to the footer for help due to missing header links; English academic terms (e.g., "Fellow") caused cognitive friction.

5 FINAL PROTOTYPE ITERATIONS

In response to spontaneous behaviors and friction points observed during testing, I implemented 5 structural enhancements on the final prototype, focusing on scannability, menu hierarchy, and overall accessibility:

Scannability & Tooltips — BEFORE (As-Is) vs AFTER (To-Be)
Enhancement made: Added purposeful bold keywords and hover tooltips over industry jargon to overcome language barriers and support scannable reading.
Global Menu & "Get Help" Link — BEFORE (As-Is) vs AFTER (To-Be)
Enhancement made: Replaced the search magnifying glass with a "Get Help" dropdown in the header and unified the footer navigation label to "About".
Course Cards & Optimized Filters — BEFORE (As-Is) vs AFTER (To-Be)
Enhancement made: Integrated category badges onto course cards and reworded duration labels to prevent numerical anchoring misinterpretations.
Smart Access & Dashboard Support
Enhancement made: Added a direct help link inside the user dashboard (My Dashboard) and implemented automatic redirection back to the originating page post-login.
Brand Humanization & Live Chat Overlay
Enhancement made: Introduced a dedicated founder spotlight in the About section and consolidated the centered, dimmed modal overlay for live chat.

TRADE-OFFS & CONSCIOUS BOUNDARIES

Every trade-off was guided by strategic priorities, testing constraints, and empirical field data, avoiding visual distractions or scope creep.

Product Scope:
I focused efforts strictly on information architecture, core usability, and an internal LMS, deliberately excluding marketing campaigns, commercial proposals, or new course development.

Live Chat Evolution:
Following layout overlaps identified during the Pilot Test on smaller screens, I removed the floating bottom-right chat bubble and replaced it with a centered modal overlay within "Get Help".

Testing & Sample Bias:
I recruited peers, accepting expert bias, and chose the Figma Focused mode (on UXArmy) to scale testing to 5 real users instead of 3.

QUALITATIVE IMPACT & WHAT REMAINS TODAY

Through this redesign, I aimed to demonstrate how a complex information architecture can be transformed into a seamless, inclusive learning tool, eliminating jarring redirects to external platforms and restoring full autonomy to learners.

The field research conducted and adherence to WCAG 2.1 standards represent a solid foundation of methodology and attention to detail, proving that listening to users is fundamental to crafting ethical, accessible, and truly valuable digital products.
